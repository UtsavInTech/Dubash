# Voice model survey for Phase C

Research checked 17 October 2026. This is a model and product survey, not a recommendation to clone any speaker without consent. A published language list or a vendor demo does not establish intelligibility for Dubash’s event audio; the short list below is a test plan, not an approval to deploy.

## What matters for Dubash

Dubash would synthesize short translated phrases after the speaker finishes a phrase. The system must preserve speaker identity across languages, produce clear native-language pronunciation, stream audio progressively, and fit within a 2–4 second speaker-to-audience delay. End-to-end latency includes speech endpointing, recognition, translation, synthesis and phone playback. A vendor’s first-byte figure covers only part of that path.

The codes in the language table are `hi` Hindi, `ta` Tamil, `kn` Kannada, `te` Telugu, `ml` Malayalam, `mr` Marathi, `bn` Bengali and `en` English. “Yes” means the vendor/model documents that language for cloning or synthesis. “Partial” means a documented subset or indirect route; “—” means no direct documented support. Clone language support is distinct from ordinary fixed-voice TTS support.

## Shortlist for a controlled Phase C trial

1. **Sarvam Instant Voice Cloning** is the first operational candidate. It covers all eight target codes, accepts a short clean clip, can clone across those languages, and streams over HTTP or WebSocket. Before any event trial, Dubash should collect a speaker-recorded confirmation statement naming Dubash and the allowed uses; record an independent opt-in for cloning; expose immediate revocation; delete both the local sample and the provider-side saved voice on withdrawal; retain a provider voice ID only while consent is active; label every generated caption/audio stream “synthetic voice”; and keep a neutral, non-cloned fallback ready. Its commercial licence still requires proof of rights and the current account’s terms. [Sarvam consent and commercial requirements](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/commercial-licensing).

2. **IndicF5** is the self-hosted research candidate where control over voice data and deployment is valuable. It covers the seven requested Indic languages except English, and its model card explicitly conditions cloning on permission. Before selecting it, measure first-audio and full-phrase latency on the target hardware and separately test Bengali, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Tamil and Telugu with native listeners; the available CPU-only paper does not prove live speed. Use a speaker-recorded authorization, separate voice-cloning consent from recognition/storage consent, implement revocation that removes reference recordings, embeddings and caches, and label every synthetic output. The team must review the gated model conditions and current MIT files before production. For English output, use a separately consented voice or a clearly disclosed neutral voice; do not imply IndicF5 supports it.

These are complementary rather than interchangeable: Sarvam is the streaming/cloud product route, while IndicF5 is the on-premises evaluation route. Neither should enter production until a measured pilot proves the whole recognition → translation → synthesis → playback path stays within Dubash’s 2–4 second goal without clipping a translated phrase or speaking content the original speaker has not finished.

## Measurement plan before choosing

Use the same consented 10–15 second reference clips and 50–100 short translated phrases per target language. Measure time-to-first-audio and complete-phrase latency at p50/p95 from the original audio endpoint, speech intelligibility with native listeners, word error rate after listening to generated audio, speaker similarity, accent/pronunciation, and failure behavior when a sentence changes or contains names/numbers. Test mixed-language proper nouns and event noise, and compare against a neutral native voice. A single global MOS should not conceal a weak language or a convincing but inaccurate phrase.
