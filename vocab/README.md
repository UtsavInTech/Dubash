The Bageshwar speaker list attaches a source URL to every entry. Several entries are spelling or inflection variants of phrases reported by news coverage, not independently measured speech-recognition errors. Warnings and rebukes can be threatening depending on context; their notes preserve that distinction. Only a subset of his public-video speech was reviewed, so the list should not be treated as an exhaustive or frequency-ranked catchphrase inventory.

## Method and known gaps

The selection emphasizes names, honorifics, devotional vocabulary, policy names, code-switching, idioms, and regional proper nouns likely to be misrecognized or mistranslated. `misheard` contains candidate phonetic or segmentation confusions, not an evaluated ASR confusion set. Review every correction against the event speaker and script before approving it.

English caption labels use common spellings for prominent names and established scheme titles where available; remaining religious and regional names use familiar roman spellings. The Kannada, Tamil, and Telugu strings are phonetic script renderings or draft expression equivalents. Only the marked high-frequency slang and selected Bageshwar phrases received direct editorial equivalents; uncertain forms carry a native-review note. Malayalam and Marathi values are supplemental drafts. Have native speakers review all non-English translations, especially jokes, insults, dialect, religious honorifics, transliteration, and scripts before live use. Comedy slang changes across regions and age groups, and some items are vulgar or stereotype-sensitive.

The regional list is a starter set, not a complete gazetteer. Scheme names and office holders change; verify current official terminology before an event. No list has been tested against a live speaker recording or an event-specific script.
